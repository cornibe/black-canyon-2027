Region and town overview

The trip sits at the northern edge of metro Phoenix, where suburban convenience quickly gives way to saguaro desert, foothills, reservoirs and historic mining and ranching towns.

Your base

Anthem

Anthem is an unincorporated master-planned community founded in 1999 in north-central Maricopa County. It has more than 27,000 residents, major parks, groceries, restaurants and direct access to I-17. It is practical rather than historic, but ideal for this race because the shuttle hub and hotel are together.

The landscape

Upper Sonoran Desert

Anthem lies where the Phoenix basin begins rising toward the Bradshaw Mountains. Expect saguaros, palo verde, creosote, rocky washes and broad mountain views. The race travels through more remote high-desert terrain north of town before descending toward the finish.

Nearby towns

Cave Creek, Black Canyon City and Wickenburg

Cave Creek mixes preserved desert foothills with a compact Western-flavored commercial center. Black Canyon City is a small I-17 community tied closely to the race corridor. Wickenburg is the most substantial historic town, with ranching, railroad and mining heritage.

February climate

February is one of the most comfortable times to visit the Phoenix region, but race-day conditions can span cold pre-dawn hours, warm exposed afternoons and a rapid cooldown after sunset.

What the family should expect

Most days are sunny and mild, with cool mornings that may require a fleece or light jacket. Desert sun remains strong even when the air feels comfortable. Rain is uncommon but possible, and a passing winter system can bring wind, clouds and substantially cooler temperatures.

LayersSun protectionWaterLight rain shell

What it means for the runner

The Mayer start and higher northern course sections can be colder than Phoenix or Anthem. Carry disposable or packable layers for the start. Later, exposed trail can feel warm and very dry. A finish bag should include insulated clothing because body temperature can drop quickly after stopping.

Indoor attractions

Indoor attractions

Useful for rain, wind, an unusually cold day or a low-energy recovery outing. Both are within approximately 45 minutes of the Anthem hotel in normal traffic.

Best indoor family choice

Musical Instrument Museum

A large, polished museum presenting music and instruments from around the world through video, audio and thousands of objects. The Experience Gallery lets visitors play instruments, making this much more engaging for a six-year-old than a conventional display-only museum. The museum is open daily, currently 9:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m., and offers free parking.

Dining options

Independent vegetarian-friendly dining

The immediate Anthem area is chain-heavy, but these locally operated restaurants provide practical vegetarian choices. Confirm sauces, stocks, beans and rice when strict vegetarian preparation matters.

Best convenient dinner

Nara Thai Cafe

Very close to the hotel and useful for a low-stress Friday meal. Vegetable, tofu, noodle, curry and fried-rice dishes should provide several options. Ask specifically whether curry paste, soup broth and sauces contain fish sauce, shrimp paste or oyster sauce.
